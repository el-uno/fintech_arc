# Money that crosses a border in seconds, and the accounting that survives it

Arc models the backend of a payments business moving value between Europe and Africa: multi-currency virtual accounts in, an instant swap to stablecoin, chain-agnostic settlement, a local-rail payout out. Three surfaces — consumer, enterprise, and a white-label **Last Mile API** — over one set of rails.

This site is two documents braided together. One is the **architecture** of that system, down to the individual ledger entries. The other is a **primer on how stablecoin payments actually work in 2026** — the economics, the regulation, and the parts that don't work — because you cannot evaluate the architecture without the second.

<div className="arc-claim">
  **This is a simulation, not a financial system.** No real funds, no bank connections, no
  blockchain. Every external integration is a deterministic simulator, and the sanctions list is
  synthetic — no real person or entity appears in any data. Nothing here is investment, legal, or
  compliance advice.
</div>

## Why this corridor

Sub-Saharan Africa is the most expensive place on earth to send money to. The World Bank puts the average cost of a $200 remittance to the region at **8.78%** against a UN Sustainable Development Goal target of 3% — and banks, the most expensive channel, at 9.50%.

Meanwhile the same region absorbed **$205bn** of on-chain value in the year to June 2025, up 52%, with stablecoins now roughly **43%** of its crypto transaction volume. The demand is not speculative. It is people and businesses routing around correspondent banking.

## What Arc is really about

A transfer that settles in seconds is easy. A transfer that settles in seconds **and cannot lose a cent when the fourth of five steps fails** is the actual engineering problem.

<div className="arc-claim">
  For every failure at every step of the settlement saga, the ledger ends balanced in every currency
  and the sender's balance is exactly what it was.
</div>

That single sentence is the strongest correctness claim this project makes, and it is asserted by a chaos suite that fails each saga step in turn. The suite was then mutation-tested — deliberately broken code introduced to check the tests actually catch it. One mutation passed all sixteen tests, which is the subject of [the best story on this site](/stories/the-journal-that-balanced-and-lied).

## Build status

Arc is built in phases, and this table is kept honest rather than aspirational.

| Phase | Scope                                                                     | Status      |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| 0     | Workspace, money primitives, event bus + outbox, boundary enforcement, CI | Complete    |
| 1     | Double-entry ledger, balances, invariant suite                            | Complete    |
| 2     | Accounts and multi-currency virtual accounts                              | Complete    |
| 3     | Chain-agnostic adapter + deterministic simulator                          | Complete    |
| 4     | Money movement: rails, quotes, the settlement saga, reversals             | Complete    |
| 5     | Risk & compliance: KYC/KYB, sanctions, AML, review queues                 | In progress |
| 6     | Platform: auth, gateway, webhooks, observability, secrets                 | Planned     |
| 7     | Partner platform, sandbox, Last Mile API, SDKs                            | Planned     |
| 8     | Reconciliation, reporting, runbooks                                       | Planned     |

Pages describing planned work say so at the top. Nothing on this site claims a capability the repository does not have.
